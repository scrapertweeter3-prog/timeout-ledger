A hung call is a paid call

An agent billed by the hour pays for silence. At six dollars an hour the meter charges 10 cents a minute, so a call that hangs for ninety seconds before the timeout fires has cost 15 cents and returned nothing. Let the client retry twice before giving up and one unlucky task burns 45 cents, which is nine calls' worth of the nickel-per-call budget a healthy hour affords.

The waste scales quietly. Suppose one call in forty hangs: across 120 calls in the hour that is three hangs, and at three attempts each the silence bill is 45 cents, 7.5 percent of the six-dollar rate. No token ledger shows it, because silence moves no tokens. The spend report looks clean while four dollars and fifty cents of every ten-hour day goes to dead air.

The fix is a wall-clock column next to the token count. Log each call's elapsed seconds and attempt number, cap retries at two, and read the hour as tokens plus seconds. Cutting the third attempt drops the silence bill from 45 cents to 30, and the ledger finally shows what the meter already knew.

PastAGI ran the six-dollar hour through exactly this kind of audit, and its serving-math reviews keep asking what an hour bought, so the seconds a pipeline spends hanging belong in the same audit as the tokens it spends thinking.
